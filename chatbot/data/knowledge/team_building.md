# Team building

## Team building basics
A party holds up to six Pokémon. A good team covers each other's weaknesses: if three of your Pokémon are weak to Electric, one Electric attacker can sweep you. Pick a mix of types, make sure you can hit most types super effectively, and include both physical and special attackers so one defensive Pokémon can't wall your whole team.

## Type coverage
Coverage means having moves that hit many types super effectively. Each Pokémon should usually carry moves of its own types (for STAB) plus one or two coverage moves of other types, such as Ice Beam on a Water type to hit Dragon and Grass types. Combinations like Ice and Electric or Fighting and Ghost hit almost every type at least neutrally.

## Team roles
Common roles are: physical attacker (high Attack), special attacker (high Special Attack), fast sweeper (high Speed, often with a setup move like Dragon Dance), tank or wall (high HP and defenses to absorb hits), support (status moves, healing, screens like Reflect), and pivot (moves like U-turn or Volt Switch to switch safely). A balanced team usually has two or three attackers, one or two bulky Pokémon and some utility.

## Defensive cores
A core is a small group of Pokémon whose types cover each other's weaknesses. The classic Fire, Water and Grass core works because each type resists what threatens the others. The Dragon, Steel and Fairy core is another strong combination. Building around a core makes it easier to switch into attacks safely.

## Team tips for the main story
Use Pokémon you enjoy and catch a variety early, including a Flying or Bug type for the first routes. Keep your team's levels close to the next gym leader's strongest Pokémon, bring healing items, and check the leader's type so you can bring a Pokémon with a type advantage. Using the Exp. Share keeps the whole team leveled. Your starter usually covers one gym well, so add types that cover its weaknesses.

## Beating gyms and the Elite Four
Before a gym, check its type and bring at least two Pokémon that resist it or hit it super effectively. For the Elite Four, aim for levels close to their aces, stock up on Hyper Potions, Full Restores and Revives, and make sure your team can handle each member's type. The champion usually has a mixed team, so balanced coverage matters most there.

## Nuzlocke challenge
A Nuzlocke is a fan-made challenge: you may only catch the first Pokémon you meet on each route, a Pokémon that faints counts as dead and must be released or boxed, and you nickname everything. Tips: favor bulky Pokémon, check boss teams in advance, avoid risky critical-hit situations, and keep a reserve box of trained Pokémon to replace losses.

## Competitive battling basics
Competitive battles usually use level 50 (official VGC doubles) or level 100 (fan singles formats). Players plan EV spreads, natures and held items, often with each item used once per team. Strong teams have a clear plan, such as weather, Trick Room or setup sweepers, plus answers to common threats. Official VGC formats change each season, so check the current rules.

## Example of a balanced in-game team
A balanced team might include: a Fire or Water starter as the main attacker, a Flying type for speed and Ground immunity, an Electric type to handle Water and Flying opponents, a Ground or Rock type to handle Electric and Fire, a Grass or Fairy type for coverage, and a bulky Pokémon like Snorlax or Lapras to absorb hits. Adjust it to the Pokémon available in your game.
