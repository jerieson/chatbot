# Game mechanics

## Type effectiveness
Every move and every Pokémon has a type. A super effective hit deals 2x damage, a not very effective hit deals 0.5x, and some types are immune (0x), for example Ghost is immune to Normal and Fighting moves. Against a dual-type Pokémon the two multipliers are multiplied together, so a move can be 4x effective (Rock against Charizard) or 0.25x. Ask "What is Fire weak against?" to see a full matchup.

## STAB (Same Type Attack Bonus)
When a Pokémon uses a move that matches one of its own types, the move gets a 1.5x damage bonus called STAB. A Water-type using Surf gets STAB; a Water-type using Thunderbolt does not. The ability Adaptability raises the bonus to 2x, and in Scarlet and Violet a Pokémon that Terastallizes into one of its original types also gets 2x.

## Physical and special moves
Damaging moves are either physical or special. Physical moves use the attacker's Attack against the target's Defense; special moves use Special Attack against Special Defense. Since Generation 4 the category belongs to each move (before that it depended on the move's type). Status moves deal no direct damage and instead change stats, cause conditions or set up effects.

## Base stats
Each species has six base stats: HP, Attack, Defense, Special Attack, Special Defense and Speed. Their sum is the base stat total; most fully evolved Pokémon are around 450-550, pseudo-legendaries have exactly 600, and many legendaries have 580-720. A Pokémon's actual stats also depend on its level, IVs, EVs and nature.

## IVs (Individual Values)
IVs are hidden values from 0 to 31 for each stat, fixed when you catch or hatch a Pokémon. Higher IVs mean slightly higher stats (up to 31 extra points at level 100). In newer games the Judge function shows them, and Hyper Training with Bottle Caps can max them out for high-level Pokémon. Breeding with a Destiny Knot passes five IVs from the parents.

## EVs (Effort Values)
EVs are points a Pokémon earns in specific stats by defeating other Pokémon (each species gives EVs in certain stats). A Pokémon can have at most 510 EVs in total and 252 in one stat; every 4 EVs adds 1 stat point at level 100. Vitamins like Protein and Carbos add EVs, feathers and wings add small amounts, and certain berries remove them, which lets you plan an EV spread.

## Natures
Each Pokémon has one of 25 natures. Most natures raise one stat by 10% and lower another by 10% (for example Adamant raises Attack and lowers Special Attack; Timid raises Speed and lowers Attack). Five natures are neutral. Since Sword and Shield, mints change a Pokémon's nature stat effect. HP is never affected by natures.

## Abilities
Abilities are passive effects, such as Levitate (immune to Ground moves) or Intimidate (lowers the opponent's Attack on entry). Each species has one or two regular abilities and often a hidden ability. An Ability Capsule switches between regular abilities, and an Ability Patch changes a Pokémon to its hidden ability.

## Critical hits
A critical hit deals 1.5x damage (2x before Generation 6) and ignores the attacker's negative stat changes and the target's positive defensive changes. The base chance is about 1 in 24 in recent games. Moves with a high critical-hit ratio (like Slash), the item Scope Lens, the ability Super Luck and the move Focus Energy raise the chance.

## Speed and priority
The Pokémon with higher Speed normally moves first; ties are random. Priority moves such as Quick Attack, Extreme Speed and Protect go before normal moves regardless of Speed. Trick Room reverses the order for five turns so slower Pokémon move first. Paralysis and some items and abilities change Speed.

## Stat stages
Moves like Swords Dance (+2 Attack) or Growl (-1 Attack) change stat stages from -6 to +6. Each positive stage multiplies the stat: +1 is 1.5x, +2 is 2x, up to +6 at 4x; negative stages divide it the same way. Accuracy and evasion have their own stages. Stat stages reset when the Pokémon switches out.

## Accuracy and evasion
A move's accuracy is its chance to hit, modified by the user's accuracy stage and the target's evasion stage. Some moves never miss (like Swift or Aerial Ace). Moves with lower accuracy, such as Fire Blast (85%) or Hydro Pump (80%), are usually stronger.

## Status conditions
Burn halves the damage of physical moves and deals 1/16 of max HP each turn. Poison deals 1/8 per turn, while bad poison (Toxic) deals increasing damage. Paralysis halves Speed and sometimes prevents the Pokémon from moving. Sleep lasts a few turns, and freeze prevents moving until it thaws (20% chance each turn). A Pokémon can only have one of these at a time. Confusion is temporary and can make a Pokémon hit itself.

## Weather
Rain boosts Water moves by 50% and weakens Fire moves. Harsh sunlight boosts Fire moves and weakens Water moves. Sandstorm damages Pokémon that are not Rock, Ground or Steel each turn and raises Rock types' Special Defense. Hail damaged non-Ice types in older games; in Scarlet and Violet it was replaced by Snow, which raises Ice types' Defense. Weather usually lasts five turns, or eight with the matching rock item.

## Held items
Each Pokémon can hold one item. Leftovers restores 1/16 of max HP every turn. Choice Band, Choice Specs and Choice Scarf boost Attack, Special Attack or Speed by 50% but lock the user into one move. Life Orb boosts damage by 30% at the cost of 10% HP per hit. Focus Sash lets a Pokémon at full HP survive one hit with 1 HP. Eviolite boosts the defenses of Pokémon that can still evolve.

## Catching Pokémon
To catch a wild Pokémon, lower its HP (ideally to red) and give it a status condition: sleep and freeze help most, paralysis, poison and burn help a little. Better balls improve the odds: Great Ball 1.5x, Ultra Ball 2x, Quick Ball is strong on the first turn, Dusk Ball works well at night and in caves, and Timer Ball improves as the battle goes on. Each species has a catch rate; legendaries are much harder to catch. The Master Ball never fails.

## Experience and levels
Pokémon gain experience by battling and level up to a maximum of 100. The Exp. Share gives experience to the whole party in newer games. A Rare Candy raises a Pokémon by one level, and Exp. Candies give fixed amounts of experience in Sword, Shield, Scarlet and Violet. Leveling up raises stats and can teach new moves or trigger evolution.

## Friendship
Friendship (happiness) rises when you walk with, level up, groom or battle with a Pokémon, and when it holds a Soothe Bell or was caught in a Luxury Ball. It falls when the Pokémon faints often or is given bitter medicine. Some Pokémon evolve at high friendship, such as Pichu, Golbat, Eevee (into Espeon or Umbreon) and Riolu.

## Evolution methods
Pokémon evolve in several ways: reaching a level, using an evolution stone, trading (sometimes while holding an item), leveling up with high friendship, at a certain time of day, in a specific location, while knowing a certain move, or by a special condition. An Everstone stops evolution, and pressing B during the evolution animation cancels it. Ask "How does Eevee evolve?" for a specific Pokémon.

## Breeding and eggs
Two compatible Pokémon (same Egg Group, or any Pokémon with Ditto) can produce an egg at a Day Care, Nursery or, in Scarlet and Violet, during a picnic. The baby is the base form of the mother's species (or of the non-Ditto parent). An Everstone passes down the holder's nature and a Destiny Knot passes five IVs. Babies can inherit egg moves from their parents.

## Shiny Pokémon
Shiny Pokémon are rare alternate-colored versions of a species with no stat differences. The base odds are 1 in 4096 since Generation 6 (1 in 8192 before). The Shiny Charm, the Masuda Method (breeding Pokémon from different-language games), mass outbreaks and Sparkling Power sandwiches in Scarlet and Violet improve the odds.

## Terastallization
Terastallization is the battle mechanic of Scarlet and Violet. Once per battle a Pokémon can Terastallize, changing its type to its Tera Type. Moves of the Tera Type get STAB, and if the Tera Type matches one of its original types the bonus rises to 2x. The Tera Orb recharges at a Pokémon Center.

## Dynamax and Gigantamax
Dynamax is the battle mechanic of Sword and Shield. For three turns a Pokémon grows huge, its HP is doubled and its moves become powerful Max Moves with extra effects. Some species have a Gigantamax form with a unique appearance and G-Max Move. It can only be used in certain places, such as gym stadiums and Max Raid Battles.

## Mega Evolution
Mega Evolution was introduced in Pokémon X and Y. A Pokémon holding its Mega Stone can Mega Evolve once per battle when the trainer has a Key Stone, gaining a new form with higher stats and sometimes a new type or ability. It returns in Pokémon Legends: Z-A.

## Z-Moves
Z-Moves were the battle mechanic of Sun, Moon, Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon. A Pokémon holding a Z-Crystal can turn one of its moves into a powerful Z-Move once per battle. Some species have exclusive Z-Moves, such as Pikachu's Catastropika.
